OPD Billing — Journey-based Reception Flow

Prototype v3 · summary for discussion · October 2026

In one line: instead of moving between screens, the receptionist is guided through each visit by short, fixed “stories”. The system asks the next question, takes the payment and records everything, so fewer clicks, fewer mistakes and the same steps every time.

1. The idea in brief

  • Stories, not screens. Each visit is split into a few stories (e.g. Visit invoicing, Orders invoicing, Invoices / Refund). A story appears only when it is needed, the receptionist works on one at a time and can switch freely.
  • Fixed stages. Every story has the same stages in every scenario, shown as a progress bar. This gives the developers one clear specification per story. A built-in Story map lists them all.
  • Guided questions. Each step is a plain yes / no question with the amount shown. The common choice (e.g. Take payment at the desk) is always the main button.

2. The stories

StoryWhat it does
Walk-in visitFrom home: choose the patient, then visit details, optional orders, payer (with eligibility), and the visit is created.
PayerNPHIES eligibility check, table of benefits, manual payer search, policy details. Locked once anything is invoiced.
Visit invoicingConsultation approval, bill, collect, See the doctor, with the doctor. A patient who leaves can be marked LWBS.
Orders invoicingDoctor’s orders, approval, bill, collect. Locked until the visit is invoiced and the doctor has started.
Add ordersReception adds tests and packages that need no doctor, with trending suggestions. Billed to the patient at the cash price.
Invoices / RefundAll invoices of the visit in one place. Refund is allowed only on lines not yet performed.
ReplaceMove an invoice between cash and insurance once approvals arrive. Only the difference is collected or refunded.
Package bookingBook the procedures of a paid package as separate visits at no charge. The next procedure is suggested.
Credit settlementSettle invoices left on credit, across the patient’s visits.

3. What the receptionist sees

  • Home: search by name, MRN, mobile or doctor. Choosing a patient offers two cards: Today’s pending actions and Walk-in visit.
  • Patient profile: pending actions split into Today · Upcoming · Past, and every visit with a visit number and a date-range filter.
  • Payments: split across several modes (Span, Visa, Mastercard, Cash, advance pots, credit). Cash is counted by notes with change shown. A short cash payment is blocked. Part payment with the rest on credit is supported.
  • Visit history: every event, tagged by story and user. Staff can leave comments and pin important ones to the top.

4. Controls built in

  • Payer can only change before invoicing; after that, changes go through Replace.
  • Orders cannot be billed before the doctor sees the patient, even orders added at reception.
  • Refunds only on lines not performed and not claimed. Performed status is shown on the invoice.
  • Internal payers (charity, zakat, staff) are approved on their own reference. Insurers follow the normal approval.
  • Every action is recorded with who did it and when.

5. Points to agree in the meeting

  • Real NPHIES eligibility response and our table of benefits layout.
  • Link to the payer master (manual payer search) and the internal payer list.
  • Order catalogue and how “trending” is defined. Should any no-doctor package go to insurance?
  • Refund policy for LWBS patients. Patient type when a cash visit’s consultation is replaced to insurance.
  • Visit number format. Should credit settlement be per patient or per visit?
